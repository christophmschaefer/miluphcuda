\begin{abstract}
\noindent This document describes the equations solved by miluphcuda and their discretization: the governing
equations of hydrodynamics and continuum mechanics, the equations of state, the strength, fragmentation and porosity
models, the SPH discretization including the tensorial correction, the time integrators and the self-gravity. The
compile-time switches that select the individual parts are given in \code{typewriter} font; they are listed in
\code{doc/compile-time-options.md}. How to install and run the code, the command-line options, the file formats, and
the material config file are described in the Markdown files in \code{doc/} and in
\code{material-config/CREATE-MATERIAL-CONFIG.md}.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
%==============================================================================

miluphcuda is a smoothed particle hydrodynamics (SPH) code for fluids and for solid and porous bodies, mainly developed
for the simulation of impacts and collisions of solid, self-gravitating bodies in planetary science. It is the CUDA
port of the code miluph and runs on a single Nvidia GPU. The code and its numerics are described in
\citet{schaefer2016} and \citet{schaefer2020}; please cite both papers and the ASCL record (ascl:1911.023) if you use
the code.

The code solves either the Euler equations (\sw{HYDRO}) or the equations of continuum mechanics with deviatoric
stress (\sw{SOLID}) in one, two or three dimensions (\sw{DIM}). An arbitrary number of materials with different
equations of state and material models can interact. Self-gravity is computed with a Barnes-Hut tree.

\paragraph{Authors.} Christoph Sch\"afer and Sven Riecker wrote the basic version. Later additions, among others:
soil model (Samuel Scherrer), Johnson-Cook model (Janka Werner), porosity models (Oliver Wandel), ANEOS support,
Collins and Mohr-Coulomb strength models, low-density weakening, rk2 integrator settings (Christoph Burger),
Drucker-Prager model, Navier-Stokes equation, tensorial correction, fast integration scheme, return mapping of the
p-alpha model, kick-drift-kick integrator (Christoph Sch\"afer). See \code{README.md} for the list of developers.

\paragraph{Notation.} Particles are labelled $i$ and $j$, Greek indices denote spatial coordinates and run from 1 to
$D$ (the dimension), and the Einstein summation convention applies to them. $\vec x_{ij} = \vec x_i - \vec x_j$,
$\vec v_{ij} = \vec v_i - \vec v_j$, $r_{ij} = |\vec x_{ij}|$, and $\gradW = \partial W(r_{ij}, h)/\partial
\vec x_i$. $e$ is the specific internal energy. In miluphcuda the smoothing length $h$ is the radius of the kernel
support, i.e.\ $W(r, h) = 0$ for $r \geq h$.


%==============================================================================
\section{Governing equations}
%==============================================================================

\subsection{Conservation equations}
The continuity equation, the momentum equation and the energy equation in Lagrangian form are
\begin{align}
 \frac{\dd \varrho}{\dd t} &= - \varrho \frac{\partial v^\alpha}{\partial x^\alpha}, \label{eq:continuity}\\
 \frac{\dd v^\alpha}{\dd t} &= \frac{1}{\varrho}\frac{\partial \sigma^{\alpha\beta}}{\partial x^\beta},
  \label{eq:momentum}\\
 \frac{\dd e}{\dd t} &= \frac{1}{\varrho}\,\sigma^{\alpha\beta}\dot\varepsilon^{\alpha\beta}
  = -\frac{p}{\varrho}\frac{\partial v^\alpha}{\partial x^\alpha}
    + \frac{1}{\varrho} S^{\alpha\beta}\dot\varepsilon^{\alpha\beta}, \label{eq:energy}
\end{align}
with the stress tensor
\begin{equation}
 \sigma^{\alpha\beta} = -p\,\delta^{\alpha\beta} + S^{\alpha\beta},
\end{equation}
the pressure $p$, the deviatoric stress $S^{\alpha\beta}$ (zero for \sw{HYDRO}), and the strain rate tensor
\begin{equation}
 \dot\varepsilon^{\alpha\beta} = \frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\partial v^\alpha}{\partial x^\beta}
  + \frac{\partial v^\beta}{\partial x^\alpha}\right).
\end{equation}
The continuity equation is integrated if \sw{INTEGRATE\_DENSITY} is set; otherwise the density is computed as the
kernel sum $\varrho_i = \sum_j m_j W_{ij}$. With \sw{INTEGRATE\_DENSITY}, individual materials can still use the
kernel sum (\code{density\_via\_kernel\_sum} in the material config). The energy equation is integrated if
\sw{INTEGRATE\_ENERGY} is set.

\subsection{Constitutive equations}
For an elastic solid, the deviatoric stress evolves according to Hooke's law in rate form, made frame-indifferent
with the Jaumann rate,
\begin{equation}
 \label{eq:dSdt}
 \frac{\dd S^{\alpha\beta}}{\dd t} = 2\mu\left(\dot\varepsilon^{\alpha\beta}
   - \frac{1}{3}\delta^{\alpha\beta}\dot\varepsilon^{\gamma\gamma}\right)
   + S^{\alpha\gamma} R^{\beta\gamma} + S^{\beta\gamma} R^{\alpha\gamma},
\end{equation}
with the shear modulus $\mu$ and the rotation rate tensor
\begin{equation}
 R^{\alpha\beta} = \frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\partial v^\alpha}{\partial x^\beta}
  - \frac{\partial v^\beta}{\partial x^\alpha}\right).
\end{equation}
The pressure follows from the equation of state (Sect.~\ref{sec:eos}). The deviatoric stress is limited by the
plasticity models (Sect.~\ref{sec:plasticity}) and reduced by damage (Sect.~\ref{sec:damage}).

\subsection{Physical viscosity}
With \sw{NAVIER\_STOKES}, a viscous stress tensor
\begin{equation}
 T^{\alpha\beta} = \eta\left(\frac{\partial v^\alpha}{\partial x^\beta} + \frac{\partial v^\beta}{\partial x^\alpha}
 - \frac{2}{3}\delta^{\alpha\beta}\frac{\partial v^\gamma}{\partial x^\gamma}\right)
\end{equation}
is added to the momentum equation, with $\eta = \varrho\nu$ and either a constant kinematic viscosity $\nu$
(\sw{CONSTANT\_KINEMATIC\_VISCOSITY}) or $\nu$ from the $\alpha$ prescription of Shakura \& Sunyaev
(\sw{SHAKURA\_SUNYAEV\_ALPHA}), using the disc scale height from the material config. \sw{KLEY\_VISCOSITY} adds the
artificial bulk viscosity of \citet{2004A&A...418..325S}.

\label{sec:eos}

types are available (\code{include/pressure.h}).

\subsection{Murnaghan}
\label{sec:murnaghan}
\begin{equation}
 p = \frac{K_0}{n}\left[\left(\frac{\varrho}{\varrho_0}\right)^n - 1\right], \qquad
 c_\mathrm{s}^2 = \frac{K_0}{\varrho_0}\left(\frac{\varrho}{\varrho_0}\right)^{n-1},
\end{equation}
with the bulk modulus $K_0$ and the reference density $\varrho_0$ \citep{melosh1996impact}. For $n = 1$ this is the
liquid EOS $p = c_\mathrm{s}^2(\varrho-\varrho_0)$. For $\varrho/\varrho_0 < \eta_\mathrm{limit}$
(\code{rho\_limit}, default 0.9) the pressure is set to zero, since the material would fragment or form droplets
instead of sustaining large tension. The sound speed is limited from below by \code{cs\_limit}.

\subsection{Tillotson}
\label{sec:tillotson}
The Tillotson EOS \citep{til62,melosh1996impact} uses $\eta = \varrho/\varrho_0$, $\chi = \eta - 1$,
$z = \varrho_0/\varrho - 1$ and $x = e/(E_0\eta^2)$. In the compressed state the pressure is
\begin{equation}
 \label{eq:tillo_c}
 p_\mathrm{c} = \left(a + \frac{b}{x+1}\right)\varrho e + A\chi + B\chi^2,
\end{equation}
in the expanded state
\begin{equation}
 \label{eq:tillo_e}
 p_\mathrm{e} = a\varrho e + \left(\frac{b\varrho e}{x+1} + A\chi\,\mathrm{e}^{-\beta z}\right)
   \mathrm{e}^{-\alpha z^2}.
\end{equation}
The regimes are
\begin{itemize}
 \item $\eta < \eta_\mathrm{limit}$ and $e \leq E_\mathrm{iv}$ (cold, expanded, fragmented): $p = 0$;
 \item $\eta \geq 1$ or $e \leq E_\mathrm{iv}$: $p = p_\mathrm{c}$;
 \item $e \geq E_\mathrm{cv}$ (and $\eta < 1$): $p = p_\mathrm{e}$;
 \item $E_\mathrm{iv} < e < E_\mathrm{cv}$ (and $\eta < 1$): linear interpolation in $e$,
   $p = [(E_\mathrm{cv}-e)\,p_\mathrm{c} + (e-E_\mathrm{iv})\,p_\mathrm{e}]/(E_\mathrm{cv}-E_\mathrm{iv})$, where
   $p_\mathrm{c}$ is set to zero for $\eta < \eta_\mathrm{limit}$, so that $p$ is continuous in $e$.
\end{itemize}
The sound speed is computed consistently from the analytic derivatives,
$c_\mathrm{s}^2 = \partial p/\partial\varrho|_e + (p/\varrho^2)\,\partial p/\partial e|_\varrho$, and limited from
below by \code{cs\_limit} (default 1\,\% of $\sqrt{A/\varrho_0}$). For type 2, $e$ is clamped to $e \geq 0$ in the
EOS. If \code{polytropic\_gamma} $> 1$ is set, completely vaporized material ($e > 100 E_\mathrm{cv}$ and $\eta < 1$)
is treated as an ideal gas, $p = (\gamma-1)\varrho e$.

\subsection{ANEOS and tabulated EOS}
\label{sec:aneos}
For type 7 (and as matrix EOS for type 13) pressure and sound speed are interpolated bilinearly in a table
$p(\varrho, e)$, $c_\mathrm{s}(\varrho, e)$, which is typically generated with ANEOS (e.g.\ with the external project
miluphANEOS). Energies below the table are clamped to the lowest table energy (cold curve). Above the table maximum the
ideal-gas fallback $p = (\gamma_\mathrm{A} - 1)\varrho e$ with \code{aneos\_gamma} is used. With
\sw{MORE\_ANEOS\_OUTPUT} or \sw{ANEOS\_VAPOR\_NO\_STRENGTH}, the tables of temperature, entropy and the ANEOS phase
flag are read as well.

\paragraph{Vapour without strength.} With \sw{ANEOS\_VAPOR\_NO\_STRENGTH}, particles of ANEOS materials whose phase
flag is \code{ANEOS\_PHASE\_TWO\_PHASE\_LV} (two-phase liquid/vapour, also assigned in the ideal-gas fallback) get
$S^{\alpha\beta} = 0$ and $p \geq 0$ when the stress tensor is assembled (\code{src/stress.cu}). Since the stored
deviatoric stress is set to zero as well, a particle that leaves the two-phase region starts with zero deviatoric
stress.

\subsection{Gases}
The ideal gas (type 9), the polytropic gas (type 0), and the isothermal gases (types 3, 12) are listed in the table
above; the sound speed of the ideal gas is $c_\mathrm{s} = \sqrt{\gamma p/\varrho}$.
With \sw{REAL\_HYDRO}, negative pressures are set to zero for all materials.

\section{Strength models}
\label{sec:plasticity}
%==============================================================================

The plasticity models limit the deviatoric stress after each evaluation of the right-hand side. With the second
invariant $J_2 = \frac{1}{2}S^{\alpha\beta}S^{\alpha\beta}$ and the yield strength $Y$, the deviatoric stress is
scaled by
\begin{equation}
 S^{\alpha\beta} \rightarrow f\,S^{\alpha\beta},\qquad
 f = \min\left(\frac{Y}{\sqrt{J_2}}, 1\right)
\end{equation}
for the pressure-dependent models (Drucker-Prager, Mohr-Coulomb, Collins), and by
\begin{equation}
 f = \min\left(\frac{Y}{\sqrt{3 J_2}}, 1\right)
\end{equation}
for the von Mises model \citep{vonmises1913}. Both are radial returns onto the yield surface \citep{wilkins1964},
as also used by \citet{libersky:1991}. The pressure-dependent models compare $Y$ with $\sqrt{J_2}$, i.e.\ $Y$ is the
yield strength in shear; the von Mises model compares $Y$ with the equivalent stress $\sqrt{3J_2}$.

Note that this differs from the form $f = \min(Y^2/3J_2, 1)$ of \citet{benasp94,benz:1995}, which was used in earlier
versions of miluphcuda \citep[eq.~17]{schaefer2016}. That factor maps the stress to
$\sqrt{3J_2} = Y^2/\sqrt{3J_2^\mathrm{trial}} < Y$, i.e.\ as far inside the yield surface as the trial state was
outside. In continuous plastic flow with an elastic stress increment $\Delta$ per time step, the limited stress
then settles at about $Y - \Delta/2$ instead of $Y$, i.e.\ a softening of first order in the time step.

\subsection{Von Mises}
$Y = Y_0$ (\code{yield\_stress}) is constant. \sw{VON\_MISES\_PLASTICITY} can additionally be combined with
Drucker-Prager or Mohr-Coulomb, in which case $Y_0$ is an upper limit, $Y \rightarrow \min(Y, Y_0)$.

\subsection{Drucker-Prager}
\begin{equation}
 Y = A + 3Bp, \qquad
 A = \frac{6\,c\cos\phi}{\sqrt{3}\,(3-\sin\phi)}, \qquad B = \frac{2\sin\phi}{\sqrt{3}\,(3-\sin\phi)},
\end{equation}
with the cohesion $c$ and the angle of internal friction $\phi$, and $Y \geq 0$. Negative pressures are not capped.

\subsection{Mohr-Coulomb}
With the coefficient of internal friction $\mu_\mathrm{i} = \tan\phi$,
\begin{equation}
 Y = \begin{cases} c + \mu_\mathrm{i} p & p > 0,\\ c + p & -c < p \leq 0,\\ 0 & p \leq -c, \end{cases}
\end{equation}
and negative pressures are capped at $p \geq -c$.

\subsection{Collins}
Following \citet{collins2004} and the implementation of \citet{2015P&SS..107....3J}, the yield strength of intact
material follows the Lundborg curve \citep{lundborg1968},
\begin{equation}
 Y_\mathrm{i} = c + \frac{\mu_\mathrm{i} p}{1 + \mu_\mathrm{i} p/(Y_\mathrm{M} - c)} \quad (p > 0), \qquad
 Y_\mathrm{i} = c \quad (p \leq 0),
\end{equation}
with the limit $Y_\mathrm{M}$ (\code{yield\_stress}) for $p \to \infty$. With
\sw{COLLINS\_PLASTICITY\_INCLUDE\_MELT\_ENERGY}, $Y_\mathrm{i}$ is multiplied by $(1 - e/e_\mathrm{melt})$ (and zero
for $e \geq e_\mathrm{melt}$). With \sw{FRAGMENTATION}, the strength of damaged material is
\begin{equation}
 Y_\mathrm{d} = \begin{cases} c_\mathrm{d} + \mu_\mathrm{d}p & p > 0,\\ c_\mathrm{d} + p & -c_\mathrm{d} < p \leq 0,\\
 0 & p \leq -c_\mathrm{d}, \end{cases}
\end{equation}
and the yield strength is $Y = \min[(1-D)Y_\mathrm{i} + D\,Y_\mathrm{d},\, Y_\mathrm{i}]$ with the damage $D$.
Negative pressure is released by damage, $p \rightarrow (1-D)p$, but only down to the residual tensile strength
$p_\mathrm{t} = \max(c_\mathrm{d}, p_\mathrm{tl})$ of fully damaged material ($p_\mathrm{tl}$:
\code{tensile\_pressure\_limit}): for $p < -p_\mathrm{t}$, $p$ is replaced by $\min[(1-D)p, -p_\mathrm{t}]$; pressures
$p \geq -p_\mathrm{t}$ are not changed.

\subsection{Collins simple}
\sw{COLLINS\_PLASTICITY\_SIMPLE} uses only the Lundborg curve for $p > 0$, $Y = c + p$ for $-c < p \leq 0$, $Y = 0$
below, and caps negative pressures at $p \geq -c$. It is not combined with the damage model.

\subsection{Low-density weakening}
With \sw{LOW\_DENSITY\_WEAKENING}, the cohesion (for von Mises: the yield strength; for Collins: the damaged cohesion)
is multiplied by
\begin{equation}
 f_\mathrm{ldw} = \begin{cases}
  1 & \eta \geq 1,\\
  \left(\dfrac{\eta-\eta_\mathrm{l}}{1-\eta_\mathrm{l}}\right)^{a}(1-\gamma) + \gamma & \eta_\mathrm{l} < \eta < 1,\\[2ex]
  \left(\dfrac{\eta}{\eta_\mathrm{l}}\right)^{b}\gamma & \eta \leq \eta_\mathrm{l},
 \end{cases}
\end{equation}
with $\eta = \varrho/\varrho_0$ (matrix density for porous materials) and the parameters $\eta_\mathrm{l}$, $a$, $b$,
$\gamma$ of the group \code{low\_density\_weakening}.

\subsection{Plastic strain and total strain}
For the standard models, the equivalent plastic strain is accumulated from the radial return,
\begin{equation}
 \Delta\varepsilon_\mathrm{p} = (1-f)\,\frac{\sqrt{3J_2}}{3G_\mathrm{eff}},
\end{equation}
with $J_2$ of the trial stress and the shear modulus $G_\mathrm{eff}$ with which $S$ grows in eq.~(\ref{eq:dSdt})
($\mu/\alpha$ for p-alpha materials). Independently of the plasticity model, the total integrated strain
\begin{equation}
 \frac{\dd\varepsilon_\mathrm{tot}}{\dd t} = \sqrt{\frac{2}{3}\,\dot\varepsilon'^{\alpha\beta}\dot\varepsilon'^{\alpha\beta}},
 \qquad \dot\varepsilon'^{\alpha\beta} = \dot\varepsilon^{\alpha\beta}
   - \frac{1}{3}\delta^{\alpha\beta}\dot\varepsilon^{\gamma\gamma},
\end{equation}
is integrated for \sw{SOLID} (HDF5 output \code{/total\_integrated\_strain}).

\subsection{Experimental models}
\sw{JC\_PLASTICITY} (Johnson-Cook), \sw{PURE\_REGOLITH} \citep{bui2008} and \sw{VISCOUS\_REGOLITH} are
experimental and not described here.


%==============================================================================
\section{Fragmentation}
\label{sec:damage}
%==============================================================================

With \sw{FRAGMENTATION}, brittle failure under tension is modelled with the Grady-Kipp model \citep{grady:1980} as
implemented by \citet{benz:1995}. The scalar damage $0 \leq D \leq 1$ parametrizes the influence of cracks:
$D = 0$ is intact, $D = 1$ fully damaged material that cannot sustain tension. Damage reduces the stress,
\begin{equation}
 \sigma^{\alpha\beta} = -\hat p\,\delta^{\alpha\beta} + (1-D)\,S^{\alpha\beta},\qquad
 \hat p = \begin{cases} p & p \geq 0,\\ (1-D)\,p & p < 0,\end{cases}
\end{equation}
where the reduction of $S^{\alpha\beta}$ is applied only with \sw{DAMAGE\_ACTS\_ON\_S} and the reduction of negative
pressure is done in the plasticity model for \sw{COLLINS\_PLASTICITY}. The stored $S^{\alpha\beta}$ is the undamaged
deviator; damage is applied where the stress is assembled.

The number of flaws per unit volume with activation strains below $\varepsilon$ follows the Weibull distribution
$n(\varepsilon) = k\varepsilon^m$ \citep{weibull:1939}. The flaws are distributed among the particles in a
preprocessing step \citep{benz:1995}: for flaw $j = 1, \ldots, N_\mathrm{flaw}$ a random particle gets the activation
threshold $\varepsilon_j = [j/(kV)]^{1/m}$ until each particle has at least one flaw, with the volume $V$ of the
brittle body; on average $N_\mathrm{flaw} = N\ln N$. The thresholds are part of the input file.

The local scalar strain is computed from the largest principal stress $\sigma_\mathrm{max}$ of the (damage-reduced)
stress tensor,
\begin{equation}
 \varepsilon = \frac{\sigma_\mathrm{max}}{(1-D)\,E}, \qquad E = \frac{9K\mu}{3K+\mu}.
\end{equation}
All flaws with thresholds below $\varepsilon$ are active, and the damage grows as
\begin{equation}
 \frac{\dd D^{1/D_\mathrm{dim}}}{\dd t} = n_\mathrm{act}\,\frac{c_\mathrm{g}}{h},\qquad
 c_\mathrm{g} = 0.4\sqrt{\frac{K + \nicefrac{4}{3}(1-D)\,\mu}{\varrho}},
\end{equation}
where $n_\mathrm{act}$ is the number of active flaws, $D_\mathrm{dim}$ the dimension, and $c_\mathrm{g}$ the crack
growth speed (40\,\% of the longitudinal wave speed in the damaged material). The integrated variable is
$D^{1/D_\mathrm{dim}}$. It is limited by $(n_\mathrm{act}/n_\mathrm{tot})^{1/D_\mathrm{dim}}$, so that a particle can
only be fully damaged if all its flaws are active. For porous materials, the porous damage $D_\mathrm{p}$
(Sect.~\ref{sec:palpha}) is added: $D = \min(1, D_\mathrm{t} + D_\mathrm{p})$ with the tensile damage $D_\mathrm{t}$.


%==============================================================================
\section{Porosity}
\label{sec:porosity}
%==============================================================================

\subsection{The p-alpha model}
\label{sec:palpha}
The p-alpha model \citep{herrmann1969} as formulated for SPH by \citet{2008Icar..198..242J} describes a porous
material by the distention $\alpha = \varrho_\mathrm{s}/\varrho \geq 1$, with the matrix density
$\varrho_\mathrm{s}$ and the bulk density $\varrho$. The pressure is
\begin{equation}
 P = \frac{1}{\alpha}\,P_\mathrm{s}(\alpha\varrho, e),
\end{equation}
where $P_\mathrm{s}$ is the matrix EOS: Tillotson (type 5), Murnaghan (type 6) or ANEOS (type 13), evaluated at the
matrix density.

\paragraph{Crush curves.} Compaction follows the crush curve $\alpha_\mathrm{cc}(P)$ with $\alpha_\mathrm{cc} =
\alpha_0$ for $P \leq p_\mathrm{e}$ and $\alpha_\mathrm{cc} = 1$ for $P \geq p_\mathrm{s}$. In between,
\begin{align}
 \text{style 0:}\quad \alpha_\mathrm{cc}(P) &= 1 + (\alpha_0-1)\frac{(p_\mathrm{s}-P)^2}{(p_\mathrm{s}-p_\mathrm{e})^2},\\
 \text{style 1:}\quad \alpha_\mathrm{cc}(P) &= 1 + s(\alpha_\mathrm{t}-1)\left(\frac{p_\mathrm{s}-P}{p_\mathrm{s}-p_\mathrm{e}}\right)^{n_2}
   + s(\alpha_\mathrm{e}-\alpha_\mathrm{t})\left(\frac{p_\mathrm{t}-P}{p_\mathrm{t}-p_\mathrm{e}}\right)^{n_1}\Theta(p_\mathrm{t}-P),
\end{align}
with $s = (\alpha_0-1)/(\alpha_\mathrm{e}-1)$, which rescales the curve of \citet{2008Icar..198..242J} such that
$\alpha_\mathrm{cc}(p_\mathrm{e}) = \alpha_0$. Styles 2 and 4 are experimental crush curves with hard-coded
constants (\code{src/pressure.cu}).

\paragraph{Time evolution of $\alpha$.} On the crush curve,
$\dot\alpha = (\dd\alpha/\dd P)\,\dot P$ with $P = P_\mathrm{s}(\alpha\varrho, e)/\alpha$, which gives
\citep[eq.~25]{2008Icar..198..242J}
\begin{equation}
 \label{eq:dalphadt}
 \frac{\dd\alpha}{\dd t} = \frac{\left(\dot e\,\dfrac{\partial P_\mathrm{s}}{\partial e}
   + \alpha\,\dot\varrho\,\dfrac{\partial P_\mathrm{s}}{\partial\varrho_\mathrm{s}}\right)\dfrac{\dd\alpha}{\dd P}}
   {\alpha + \dfrac{\dd\alpha}{\dd P}\left(P - \varrho\,\dfrac{\partial P_\mathrm{s}}{\partial\varrho_\mathrm{s}}\right)}.
\end{equation}
Unloading is elastic: if eq.~(\ref{eq:dalphadt}) gives $\dot\alpha > 0$ (pressure decreasing), $\dot\alpha = 0$.
With \sw{PALPHA\_ELASTIC\_RELOADING}, reloading below the crush curve is elastic as well: for
$\alpha < \alpha_\mathrm{cc}(P) - \epsilon_\mathrm{tol}$, $\dd\alpha/\dd P = 0$. Then $\alpha =
\alpha_\mathrm{cc}(P_\mathrm{max})$ keeps the memory of the maximum pressure. Without it, every pressure increase
above $p_\mathrm{e}$ compacts \citep[eqs.~22--23]{2008Icar..198..242J}.

\paragraph{Return mapping.} With \sw{PALPHA\_RETURN\_MAPPING}, $\alpha$ is not integrated. For each evaluated state,
$\alpha$ is determined from $\varrho$ and $e$ with the distention $\alpha_\mathrm{h}$ after the last accepted step:
if $g(\alpha_\mathrm{h}) > 0$ with
\begin{equation}
 g(\alpha) = \alpha - \alpha_\mathrm{cc}\!\left(P_\mathrm{s}(\alpha\varrho, e)/\alpha\right),
\end{equation}
the state is compressed beyond the crush curve and $\alpha$ is the root of $g$ in $[1, \alpha_\mathrm{h}]$ (Newton
iteration safeguarded by bisection); otherwise $\alpha = \alpha_\mathrm{h}$. Since $\alpha\,\dd g/\dd\alpha$ is the
denominator of eq.~(\ref{eq:dalphadt}), $g$ is increasing and the root unique wherever the time integration of
$\alpha$ is well defined. $\alpha$ then follows the crush curve independently of the time step. The changes of the
deviatoric stress and of the porous damage that the integration of $\dot\alpha$ would cause are applied in closed form.

\paragraph{Deviatoric stress.} With \sw{STRESS\_PALPHA\_POROSITY}, the code integrates the porous deviator
$\hat S^{\alpha\beta} = S^{\alpha\beta}/\alpha$ with the matrix deviator $S^{\alpha\beta}$
\citep[eqs.~45, 49]{2008Icar..198..242J},
\begin{equation}
 \frac{\dd\hat S^{\alpha\beta}}{\dd t} = \frac{f_\mathrm{r}}{\alpha}\,2\mu\left(\dot\varepsilon^{\alpha\beta}
   - \frac{1}{3}\delta^{\alpha\beta}\dot\varepsilon^{\gamma\gamma}\right)
   + f_\mathrm{r}\left(\hat S^{\alpha\gamma}R^{\beta\gamma} + \hat S^{\beta\gamma}R^{\alpha\gamma}\right)
   - \frac{\hat S^{\alpha\beta}}{\alpha}\frac{\dd\alpha}{\dd t},
\end{equation}
with $f_\mathrm{r} = f$ for \sw{PALPHA\_F\_ACTS\_ON\_ROTATION} = 1 (as in \citealt{2008Icar..198..242J}) and
$f_\mathrm{r} = 1$ otherwise. The factor
\begin{equation}
 f = 1 + \frac{\varrho}{\alpha}\frac{\dd\alpha}{\dd\varrho}, \qquad
 \frac{\dd\alpha}{\dd\varrho} = \frac{\left(\dfrac{P}{\varrho^2}\dfrac{\partial P_\mathrm{s}}{\partial e}
   + \alpha\dfrac{\partial P_\mathrm{s}}{\partial\varrho_\mathrm{s}}\right)\dfrac{\dd\alpha}{\dd P}}
   {\alpha + \dfrac{\dd\alpha}{\dd P}\left(P - \varrho\,\dfrac{\partial P_\mathrm{s}}{\partial\varrho_\mathrm{s}}\right)},
\end{equation}
relates the volumetric strain rate of the matrix to that of the bulk material; $f \leq 1$, and $f = 1$ for elastic
states. Physically, $f$ belongs only to the volumetric part of the strain rate, which drops out of the traceless Hooke
term; therefore \sw{PALPHA\_F\_ACTS\_ON\_ROTATION} = 0 ($f_\mathrm{r} = 1$) is recommended. It leaves the Jaumann
terms, which are pure kinematics, unscaled and reproduces the rigid rotation of a porous particle exactly.

\paragraph{Porous damage.} With \sw{FRAGMENTATION}, compaction produces damage
\citep[eq.~57]{2008Icar..198..242J},
\begin{equation}
 D_\mathrm{p}^{1/D_\mathrm{dim}} = \frac{\left(1 - \dfrac{\alpha-1}{\alpha_0-1} + \delta\right)^{1/D_\mathrm{dim}}
   - \delta^{1/D_\mathrm{dim}}}{(1+\delta)^{1/D_\mathrm{dim}} - \delta^{1/D_\mathrm{dim}}}, \qquad \delta = 0.01,
\end{equation}
which is integrated in time along with $\alpha$ (or applied exactly with the return mapping).

\paragraph{Sound speed.} The sound speed is interpolated linearly in $\alpha$ between the sound speed of the matrix
($\alpha = 1$) and $c_\mathrm{porous}$ (\code{cs\_porous}, $\alpha = \alpha_0$).

\subsection{The $\varepsilon$-$\alpha$ model}
With \sw{EPSALPHA\_POROSITY} (type 11), the distention is a function of the volumetric strain $\varepsilon_V$
\citep{2006Icar..180..514W}: elastic for $\varepsilon_V \geq \varepsilon_\mathrm{e}$, exponential compaction
$\dd\alpha/\dd\varepsilon_V = \alpha_0\kappa\,\mathrm{e}^{\kappa(\varepsilon_V-\varepsilon_\mathrm{e})}$ for
$\varepsilon_\mathrm{x} \leq \varepsilon_V < \varepsilon_\mathrm{e}$, a power law down to full compaction at
$\varepsilon_\mathrm{c}$. The pressure is $P = P_\mathrm{s}(\alpha\varrho, e)/\alpha$ with the Tillotson EOS.

\subsection{The Sirono model}
With \sw{SIRONO\_POROSITY} (type 10), the modified Sirono model of \citet{gerspe11} for highly porous dust aggregates
is used, with density-dependent compressive, tensile and shear strength.

\section{SPH discretization}
%==============================================================================

\subsection{Kernels}
The kernel is chosen at run time with \code{-k}. With $q = r/h$ and $(\cdot)_+ = \max(0,\cdot)$:

\paragraph{Cubic spline} \citep{1985A&A...149..135M}, \code{cubic\_spline} (default),
\begin{equation}
 W = \frac{\sigma}{h^D}\begin{cases} 6q^3 - 6q^2 + 1 & 0 \leq q < \frac{1}{2},\\
 2(1-q)^3 & \frac{1}{2} \leq q \leq 1, \end{cases}
 \qquad \sigma = \tfrac{4}{3},\ \tfrac{40}{7\pi},\ \tfrac{8}{\pi}.
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Quintic spline}, \code{quintic\_spline},
\begin{equation}
 W = \frac{\sigma}{h^D}\left[(3-3q)_+^5 - 6(2-3q)_+^5 + 15(1-3q)_+^5\right],
 \qquad \sigma = \tfrac{1}{40},\ \tfrac{63}{478\pi},\ \tfrac{9}{40\pi}.
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Wendland kernels} \citep{2012MNRAS.425.1068D}, \code{wendlandc2}, \code{wendlandc4}, \code{wendlandc6}, in
2D and 3D
\begin{align}
 W_\mathrm{C2} &= \frac{\sigma}{h^D}(1-q)_+^4(1+4q), & \sigma &= \tfrac{7}{\pi},\ \tfrac{21}{2\pi},\\
 W_\mathrm{C4} &= \frac{\sigma}{h^D}(1-q)_+^6\left(1+6q+\tfrac{35}{3}q^2\right), & \sigma &= \tfrac{9}{\pi},\ \tfrac{495}{32\pi},\\
 W_\mathrm{C6} &= \frac{\sigma}{h^D}(1-q)_+^8(1+8q+25q^2+32q^3), & \sigma &= \tfrac{78}{7\pi},\ \tfrac{1365}{64\pi},
\end{align}
and in 1D $(1-q)_+^3(1+3q)$, $(1-q)_+^5(1+5q+8q^2)$, $(1-q)_+^7(1+7q+19q^2+21q^3)$ with $\sigma = \frac{5}{4},
\frac{3}{2}, \frac{55}{32}$.

\paragraph{Spiky kernel} \citep{desbrun1996}, \code{spiky}, $W = \sigma h^{-D}(1-q)_+^3$ with
$\sigma = \frac{10}{\pi}, \frac{15}{\pi}$ in 2D and 3D.

The normalization constants are given for $D = 1, 2, 3$ unless noted otherwise.

\subsection{Smoothing length and neighbours}
For an interaction of particles $i$ and $j$, the kernel is evaluated with $h_{ij} = (h_i+h_j)/2$, or as
$W_{ij} = [W(r_{ij}, h_i) + W(r_{ij}, h_j)]/2$ with \sw{AVERAGE\_KERNELS}. The smoothing length is constant unless
\sw{VARIABLE\_SML} is set, in which case either the number of interaction partners is kept fixed
(\sw{FIXED\_NOI}, \citealt{hernquist:1989}) or
\begin{equation}
 \frac{\dd h_i}{\dd t} = \frac{h_i}{D}\,\nabla\cdot\vec v_i
   = -\frac{h_i}{D}\sum_j \frac{m_j}{\varrho_j}\,\vec v_{ij}\cdot\gradW
\end{equation}
is integrated (\sw{INTEGRATE\_SML}), with $h$ limited to
$[f_\mathrm{min} h_0, f_\mathrm{max} h_0]$ (\code{factor\_sml\_min}, \code{factor\_sml\_max}). The interaction
partners are found with the Barnes-Hut tree; their number is limited by \sw{MAX\_NUM\_INTERACTIONS}.

\subsection{Continuity equation}
\begin{equation}
 \frac{\dd\varrho_i}{\dd t} = \sum_j m_j\frac{\varrho_i}{\varrho_j}\,\vec v_{ij}\cdot\gradW
\end{equation}
\citep{randles:1996}. With \sw{TENSORIAL\_CORRECTION\_FOR\_DRHODT}, $\gradW$ is replaced by the corrected gradient
$\mathsf{B}_i\gradW$ (Sect.~\ref{sec:tc}), and $\varrho_j$ by $\tilde\varrho_{ij}$, eq.~(\ref{eq:tcrho}).

\subsection{Momentum equation}
\label{sec:momentum}
Two SPH representations are available (\sw{SPH\_EQU\_VERSION}),
\begin{align}
 \text{1:}\quad \frac{\dd v_i^\alpha}{\dd t} &= \sum_j m_j\left(\frac{\sigma_i^{\alpha\beta}}{\varrho_i^2}\,G_{i}^\beta
   + \frac{\sigma_j^{\alpha\beta}}{\varrho_j^2}\,G_{j}^\beta\right) - \sum_j m_j\Pi_{ij}\,\partial_{i,\alpha}W_{ij},\\
 \text{2:}\quad \frac{\dd v_i^\alpha}{\dd t} &= \sum_j m_j\,\frac{\sigma_i^{\alpha\beta} + \sigma_j^{\alpha\beta}}{\varrho_i\varrho_j}\,
   \frac{G_{i}^\beta + G_{j}^\beta}{2} - \sum_j m_j\Pi_{ij}\,\partial_{i,\alpha}W_{ij},
\end{align}
where $\vec G_{i} = \mathsf B_i\gradW$ and $\vec G_{j} = \mathsf B_j\gradW$ are the corrected kernel gradients with the
correction matrices of particles $i$ and $j$ (Sect.~\ref{sec:tc}), or $\vec G_{i} = \vec G_{j} = \gradW$ without
\sw{TENSORIAL\_CORRECTION}, and $\Pi_{ij}$ is the artificial viscosity. For \sw{HYDRO}, $\sigma^{\alpha\beta} =
-p\,\delta^{\alpha\beta}$. The stress tensor includes the reduction by damage. With \sw{ARTIFICIAL\_STRESS}, the term
$m_j f_{ij}^n (R_i^{\alpha\beta}/\varrho_i^2 G_i^\beta + R_j^{\alpha\beta}/\varrho_j^2 G_j^\beta)$ with
$f_{ij} = W(r_{ij})/W(\Delta p)$ is added \citep{monaghan:2000,gray:2001}.

\subsection{Energy equation}
The energy equation is discretized pairwise, consistent with the momentum equation: with the acceleration
$\vec a_{ij}$ of particle $i$ due to particle $j$ (the summand of the momentum equation without the artificial
viscosity),
\begin{equation}
 \frac{\dd e_i}{\dd t} = -\frac{1}{2}\sum_j \vec v_{ij}\cdot\vec a_{ij}
   + \frac{1}{2}\sum_j m_j\Pi_{ij}\,\vec v_{ij}\cdot\gradW.
\end{equation}
For \sw{SPH\_EQU\_VERSION} 1 and \sw{HYDRO} this is the standard
$\dot e_i = \frac{1}{2}\sum_j m_j (p_i/\varrho_i^2 + p_j/\varrho_j^2)\,\vec v_{ij}\cdot\gradW + \ldots$. Together with
the momentum equation the total energy is conserved.

\subsection{Velocity gradient}
The strain rate and rotation rate tensors are computed from the velocity gradient. Without tensorial correction
\citep{benz:1995},
\begin{equation}
 \frac{\partial v_i^\alpha}{\partial x^\beta} = -\frac{1}{\varrho_i}\sum_j m_j\,v_{ij}^\alpha\,\partial_{i,\beta} W_{ij},
\end{equation}
and with tensorial correction
\begin{equation}
 \frac{\partial v_i^\alpha}{\partial x^\beta} = -\sum_j \frac{m_j}{\tilde\varrho_{ij}}\,v_{ij}^\alpha\,
   \left(\mathsf B_i\gradW\right)^\beta,
\end{equation}
which is exact for linear velocity fields. $\dot\varepsilon$ and $R$ are the symmetric and antisymmetric parts.

\subsection{Artificial viscosity}
With \sw{ARTIFICIAL\_VISCOSITY} \citep{Monaghan85a},
\begin{equation}
 \Pi_{ij} = \begin{cases} \dfrac{-\alpha\,\bar c_{ij}\mu_{ij} + \beta\mu_{ij}^2}{\bar\varrho_{ij}} &
  \vec v_{ij}\cdot\vec x_{ij} < 0,\\[1.5ex] 0 & \text{otherwise,}\end{cases}
 \qquad \mu_{ij} = \frac{\bar h_{ij}\,\vec v_{ij}\cdot\vec x_{ij}}{r_{ij}^2 + \epsilon\bar h_{ij}^2},
\end{equation}
with the means $\bar c_{ij}$, $\bar\varrho_{ij}$, $\bar h_{ij}$ of the sound speeds, densities and smoothing lengths,
$\epsilon = 0.01$, and the material parameters $\alpha$, $\beta$ (default 1 and 2). With \sw{BALSARA\_SWITCH}
\citep{balsara1995}, $\mu_{ij}$ is multiplied by $(f_i+f_j)/2$,
$f_i = |\nabla\cdot\vec v_i|/(|\nabla\cdot\vec v_i| + |\nabla\times\vec v_i| + 10^{-4}c_i/h_i)$.
\sw{INVISCID\_SPH} uses time-dependent coefficients \citep{cullen2010}.

\subsection{XSPH and Shepard correction}
With \sw{XSPH} the particles move with the smoothed velocity $\dot{\vec x}_i = \vec v_i + \frac{1}{2}
\sum_j m_j/\bar\varrho_{ij}\,(\vec v_j - \vec v_i)W_{ij}$ \citep{monaghan:1989}. \sw{SHEPARD\_CORRECTION} normalizes
the kernel to zeroth-order consistency.

\subsection{Tensorial correction}
\label{sec:tc}
Standard SPH kernel gradients do not reproduce the gradient of a linear field, and solid bodies cannot rotate rigidly
without spurious stress. With \sw{TENSORIAL\_CORRECTION} \citep{randles:1996}, each particle gets the correction matrix
$\mathsf B_i = \mathsf M_i^{-1}$ with the (symmetric) moment matrix
\begin{equation}
 M_i^{\alpha\beta} = \sum_j \frac{m_j}{\tilde\varrho_{ij}}\,x_{ji}^\alpha\,\partial_{i,\beta}W_{ij},
 \qquad x_{ji}^\alpha = x_j^\alpha - x_i^\alpha,
\end{equation}
computed with the same kernel and smoothing length as the force loops, and the weight
\begin{equation}
 \label{eq:tcrho}
 \tilde\varrho_{ij} = \begin{cases} \varrho_j & \text{default \citep{randles:1996}},\\
 \varrho_i & \text{\sw{TENSORIAL\_CORRECTION\_CONSERVE\_ANGULAR\_MOMENTUM}.} \end{cases}
\end{equation}
The corrected gradient $\mathsf B_i\gradW$ makes the velocity gradient exact for linear fields. Close to free surfaces
and contact zones the moment matrix becomes ill-conditioned. With the default (``old-school'') scheme in
\code{src/kernel.cu}, the identity is used instead of $\mathsf B_i$ if $|\det\mathsf B_i| < 0.2$ or $> 5$, or if
an entry of $\mathsf B_i$ exceeds \code{MAX\_ABS\_TENSORIAL\_CORRECTION\_ENTRY} (5). With
\sw{TENSORIAL\_CORRECTION\_CONSERVE\_ANGULAR\_MOMENTUM}, the conditioning is judged on the matrix with weight
$\varrho_j$, so that a pure density contrast (e.g.\ at material interfaces) does not reset the correction.

\paragraph{Angular momentum.} With weight $\varrho_i$ and \sw{SPH\_EQU\_VERSION} 1, the pair forces of the momentum
equation are torque-free also with deviatoric stress, variable density and variable smoothing length (wherever
$\mathsf B$ is not reset to the identity), so the total angular momentum is conserved exactly by the spatial
discretization; with the \code{kdk} integrator also in time (up to round-off).

\paragraph{Continuity equation.} The correction is used in the momentum and energy equations and in the velocity
gradient for $\dot\varepsilon$ and $R$, but by default not in the continuity equation and in $\dot h$
(\sw{TENSORIAL\_CORRECTION\_FOR\_DRHODT} = 0): with the corrected divergence the density can decouple from the
particle geometry, which leads to a collapse of the time step.


%==============================================================================
\section{Time integration}
%==============================================================================

\subsection{Integrators}
The integrator is chosen with \code{-I}. All integrators advance positions, velocities, density, energy, smoothing
length, deviatoric stress, damage, distention, and plastic and total strain.

\paragraph{\code{rk2\_adaptive}} (default). Each step uses three right-hand side evaluations
$k_1 = f(y_n)$, $k_2 = f(y_n + \frac{\Delta t}{2}k_1)$, $k_3 = f(y_n - \Delta t\,k_1 + 2\Delta t\,k_2)$ and
advances with the third-order combination $y_{n+1} = y_n + \frac{\Delta t}{6}(k_1 + 4k_2 + k_3)$. The difference to
the second-order midpoint solution $y_n + \Delta t\,k_2$ is the error estimate. The maximum relative error of the
positions (relative to $\max(|\Delta\vec x|, 0.1\,h)$), velocities, densities and energies, divided by the accuracy
$\epsilon$ (\code{-Q}), decides whether the step is accepted. The new step is
$\Delta t_\mathrm{new} = 0.9\,\Delta t\,E^{-0.3}$ after an accepted and $\max(0.1, 0.9E^{-0.25})\Delta t$ after a
rejected step, with $E$ the normalized error, and is additionally limited by the Courant condition and by the damage
change (and optionally by the accelerations, the pressure change and the change of $\alpha$), see
\code{include/rk2adaptive.h}.

\paragraph{\code{kdk}} (kick-drift-kick, leapfrog). With one right-hand side evaluation per step,
\begin{align}
 \vec v^{n+1/2} &= \vec v^n + \frac{\Delta t}{2}\vec a^n, \qquad \vec x^{n+1} = \vec x^n + \Delta t\,\vec v^{n+1/2},\\
 \vec a^{n+1} &= \vec a\left(\vec x^{n+1}, \vec v^n + \Delta t\,\vec a^n, Q^n + \Delta t\,\dot Q^n\right),\qquad
 \vec v^{n+1} = \vec v^{n+1/2} + \frac{\Delta t}{2}\vec a^{n+1},
\end{align}
and the same kicks for all other integrated quantities $Q$. The right-hand side at the end of the step is evaluated at
the predicted state, as in Phantom \citep{price2018}. Since the drift uses the velocity of the kicks, the total angular
momentum is conserved to round-off for every step size if the forces are torque-free
(\sw{TENSORIAL\_CORRECTION\_CONSERVE\_ANGULAR\_MOMENTUM}, no XSPH, no boundaries, no tree gravity). The time step is set
before each step from the Courant condition and optional limits (\code{include/kdk.h}); there is no error control.

\paragraph{Fixed-order schemes.} \code{euler} (first order, fixed $\Delta t$ given with \code{-M}),
\code{euler\_pc} and \code{monaghan\_pc} (predictor-corrector schemes of second order with predictor step $\Delta t$
and $\Delta t/2$), and \code{heun\_rk4} (Heun scheme for the SPH particles coupled with a fourth-order Runge-Kutta
scheme for gravitating point masses). The predictor-corrector and Heun schemes limit the step by the Courant condition
and the accelerations.

\subsection{Courant condition}
\begin{equation}
 \Delta t \leq C\,\min_i \frac{h_i}{\sqrt{c_{\mathrm{s},i}^2 + \frac{4}{3}\mu/\varrho_i}},
\end{equation}
with $C$ = \code{COURANT\_FACT} = 0.4 (note that $h$ is the full support radius) and the shear term only for
\sw{SOLID}. The force limit is $\Delta t \leq F\min_i\sqrt{h_i/|\vec a_i|}$ with $F$ = \code{FORCES\_FACT}.

\subsection{Fast integration scheme}
With \sw{FAST\_INTEGRATION\_SCHEME} \citep{raducan2022,jutzi2022}, the late, low-velocity stage of granular flow,
where the time step is set by the high sound speed of the material although the flow is slow, can be computed with
much larger time steps. At the transition time $t_\mathrm{f}$ of a material (group \code{fast\_integration} in the
material config), its EOS is replaced by a linear medium with a low bulk modulus $K_\mathrm{f}$,
\begin{equation}
 p = K_\mathrm{f}\left(\frac{\varrho_\mathrm{s}}{\varrho_{0,\mathrm{f}}} - 1\right)
  \quad\text{(Murnaghan with $n = 1$)},\qquad c_\mathrm{s} = \sqrt{K_\mathrm{f}/\varrho_{0,\mathrm{f}}},
\end{equation}
with $\varrho_\mathrm{s} = \varrho$ for non-porous (types 1, 2, 7 $\to$ 1) and $\varrho_\mathrm{s} = \alpha\varrho$
for p-alpha materials (types 5, 6, 13 $\to$ 6, $p = K_\mathrm{f}(\alpha\varrho/\varrho_{0,\mathrm{f}} - 1)/\alpha$).
The shear modulus becomes $\mu_\mathrm{f} = 3K_\mathrm{f}(1-2\nu)/[2(1+\nu)]$ with the given or the current Poisson
ratio $\nu$, and the deviatoric stress is rescaled by $\mu_\mathrm{f}/\mu$ so that the elastic strain is unchanged.
The tension cut-off \code{rho\_limit} of the new medium defaults to 1 (no tension below $\varrho_{0,\mathrm{f}}$).
The strength model is unchanged. The scheme is valid only if the flow is slow compared to the new sound speed.


%==============================================================================
\section{Self-gravity}
\label{sec:self-gravity}
%==============================================================================

Self-gravity (\code{-s}) is computed with a Barnes-Hut tree \citep{1986Natur.324..446B} following the GPU algorithms
of \citet{burtscher:2011}. The same tree is used to find the SPH interaction partners \citep{hernquist:1989}.

\subsection{Tree construction}
The hierarchical decomposition is stored in an octree (quadtree in 2D). Since pointers are inconvenient on the GPU, one
index array \code{childList} holds all particles and tree nodes; up to $\lceil 2.5N\rceil$ nodes with $2^D$ children
each are allocated. The root node is the cube enclosing all particles. The particles are inserted in parallel: each
thread descends from the root to the leaf where its particle belongs and locks the child entry with
\code{atomicCAS} (status $-2$, ``locked''; $-1$ means ``empty''). If the entry is occupied by another particle, new
inner nodes with half the edge length are created until both particles reside in different leaves; then the lock is
released. Afterwards, the masses and centres of mass of all inner nodes (pseudo-particles) are computed bottom-up.

\subsection{Gravitational force}
For each particle the tree is walked from the root. A node of edge length $s$ at distance $r$ is used as a whole if
\begin{equation}
 s/r < \vartheta,
\end{equation}
otherwise its children are opened. The acceleration from a pseudo-particle of mass $m_p$ at $\vec x_p$ is
$\vec a_i = -G m_p\,(\vec x_i - \vec x_p)/r^3$, with a smoothed distance to avoid singularities. Only monopoles are
used; therefore $\vartheta \leq 0.5$ (\code{-a}, default 0.5) is recommended \citep{1989ApJS...70..389B}. Direct
summation is available with \code{-D}.

\subsection{Decoupled gravity}
With \code{-g}, the tree gravity is not recomputed in every right-hand side evaluation. It is recomputed every tenth
evaluation, or earlier if more than 0.1\,\% of the particles have changed their tree node since the last computation;
in between, the stored gravitational accelerations are reused. This saves time if the hydrodynamic time step is much
shorter than the gravitational time scale. The criterion is under revision.

\subsection{Point masses}
With \sw{GRAVITATING\_POINT\_MASSES}, point masses interact gravitationally with each other and with the SPH particles
(including the back reaction). SPH particles closer than $r_\mathrm{min}$ or farther than $r_\mathrm{max}$ are removed,
or accreted with \sw{PARTICLE\_ACCRETION} and \sw{UPDATE\_SINK\_VALUES}.
